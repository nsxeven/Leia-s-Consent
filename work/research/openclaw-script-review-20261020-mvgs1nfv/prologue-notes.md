# Prologue Script Review: Character, Pacing, and Choice

**Source:** `game/chapters/prologue.rpy`  
**Inspected:** Lines 1–418 (through EOF)

## Critique

The prologue succeeds as a character study of three people who already know each other too well. Ryan's voice is observant but reactive—he notices things but rarely acts on them until forced by circumstance. Leia is the counterweight: patient, strategically quiet, and self-aware enough to know when she's performing. Julian exists as comic relief and catalyst, his presence justifying the "first trip" framing.

The narrative structure is deliberate: name setup (1–50) grounds the player, then "Four Years Ago" (51–150) compresses their first meeting into a single night. The kitchen scene (151–200) provides the only quiet pocket of time before Truth or Dare escalates into public spectacle. This works, but risks feeling like a single extended beat rather than a series of discoveries.

The kiss (201–250) is handled with charming awkwardness—nearly colliding noses, a room that "cheered like we'd accomplished something." It feels earned because the room had already turned against her. The present-day framing (301–418) bookends the memory with packing banter and Julian's looming presence, creating a sense of cyclical time.

**Strengths:** Leia's dialogue carries the most weight. She's the one who explains, who retreats and returns, who says "Recharging" as a deflection. Ryan's lines are shorter, more reactive. The kitchen scene's silence ("the silence between sentences felt easy instead of awkward") is the prologue's emotional core.

**Weaknesses:** Ryan's internal monologue occasionally over-explains. "She wasn't shy exactly" (138) tells rather than shows. The Truth or Dare sequence accelerates quickly, and while the kiss lands, the room's reaction feels slightly generic. Julian's role is clear but underutilized—he's present but not fully integrated into the emotional arc.

## Three Prioritized Improvements

### 1. Deepen Ryan's Internal Voice (Lines 65–140)
**Proposal:** Replace 2–3 instances of Ryan's explanatory narration with reactive dialogue or physical action. Let the kitchen scene's silence do more work.
**Why:** Ryan currently narrates his own observations ("I noticed [girlfriend_name] before I ever spoke to her"). Giving him more to *do* rather than *say* would make his eventual decisions feel more earned.

### 2. Extend the Kitchen Beat (Lines 151–200)
**Proposal:** Add 1–2 lines of shared physical action—perhaps Leia reaching for the same bottle, or Ryan mirroring her posture. This would make the "quiet pocket" feel more intentional.
**Why:** The kitchen scene is the prologue's emotional anchor but risks feeling like a single block. Breaking it with small gestures would make the transition to Truth or Dare feel like an escalation rather than a switch.

### 3. Sharpen Julian's Entrance (Lines 301–350)
**Proposal:** Give Julian one line of direct interaction before the phone notifications take over. A quick "You're both ready" or "Don't forget the flight" would ground him in the moment.
**Why:** Julian is the catalyst for everything, yet his presence is mostly reported through Ryan's narration and phone UI. A direct line would make the "trip" feel more like a true three-way dynamic.

---

**Evidence:** Lines 1–418 read consecutively, with the name setup establishing voice, the kitchen scene providing contrast, and the present-day framing creating cyclical momentum. The script balances exposition with playable moments, though Ryan's internal monologue occasionally competes with Leia's more natural delivery.
